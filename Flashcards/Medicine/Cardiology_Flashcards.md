# Cardiology Flashcards

What happens to the JVP y descent in Cardiac Tamponade vs Constrictive Pericarditis?
?
- **Cardiac Tamponade:** **Blunted or absent y descent** (prominent x descent).
- **Constrictive Pericarditis:** **Prominent y descent** (and prominent x descent + Kussmaul sign + pericardial knock).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9647251 -->

What is Beck's triad of cardiac tamponade?
?
1. Muffled heart sounds
2. Hypotension / Low blood pressure
3. Raised Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)
<!-- QBank ID: Q4861198 -->

What is pulsus paradoxus?
?
An inspiratory drop in systolic blood pressure > 10 mmHg (classic sign of cardiac tamponade).
<!-- QBank ID: Q4861198 -->

Which organism causes late prosthetic valve endocarditis (>= 12 months post-surgery)?
?
**Viridans streptococci** (resembles native valve endocarditis).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6471501 -->

Which organism causes early prosthetic valve endocarditis (< 12 months post-surgery)?
?
**Coagulase-negative staphylococci** (*Staphylococcus epidermidis*).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6471501 -->

What colonic condition is associated with *Streptococcus gallolyticus* (*bovis*) endocarditis?
?
**Colorectal carcinoma** (requires colonoscopy evaluation).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6471501 -->

What is the most common major manifestation of Acute Rheumatic Fever?
?
**Migratory polyarthritis**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8408265 -->

Is a prolonged PR interval a major or minor Jones criterion for Rheumatic Fever?
?
It is a **minor criterion**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8408265 -->

What is the first cardiac chamber to enlarge on chest radiograph in Mitral Stenosis?
?
The **Left Atrium (LA)** (gives straight left heart border, double density sign, and splayed carina).
<!-- QBank ID: Q2782537 -->

How does the S2-Opening Snap (S2-OS) interval relate to the severity of Mitral Stenosis?
?
A **shorter S2-OS interval** (and longer diastolic murmur) indicates **more severe (tighter) stenosis**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2782537 -->

What is an Austin-Flint murmur in severe Aortic Regurgitation?
?
A soft, low-pitched **apical mid-diastolic rumble** without loud S1 or opening snap, caused by the aortic regurgitant jet striking the anterior mitral leaflet.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7832066 -->

What is the survival timeline for the cardinal triad of Aortic Stenosis once symptoms appear?
?
- **Angina:** ~5 years
- **Syncope:** ~3 years
- **Heart Failure / Dyspnea:** ~2 years (worst prognosis; mandates urgent AVR)
<!-- QBank ID: Q6659316 -->

What classic ECG findings define Acute Pericarditis?
?
**Widespread concave (saddle-shaped) ST elevation** with **PR-segment depression**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8140031 -->

What is the clinical presentation of Constrictive Pericarditis?
?
Right-heart failure (ascites, edema), **Kussmaul sign** (inspiratory JVP rise), **Pericardial knock**, and **prominent x and y descents** on JVP.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9558672 -->

# 📋 Criteria Breakdown Flashcards

What are the 5 MAJOR criteria of the Modified Jones Criteria for Rheumatic Fever (JONES)?
?
- **J** - Joint involvement (Migratory polyarthritis)
- **O** - Ophthalmic/Carditis (Pancarditis)
- **N** - Nodules (Subcutaneous nodules)
- **E** - Erythema marginatum
- **S** - Sydenham chorea
<!-- QBank ID: Q8408265 -->

What are the 4 MINOR criteria of the Modified Jones Criteria for Rheumatic Fever (PEACE)?
?
- **P** - Prolonged PR interval
- **E** - Elevated ESR / CRP
- **A** - Arthralgia
- **F** - Fever / Temperature elevation
<!-- QBank ID: Q8408265 -->

What are the diagnostic requirements for Acute Rheumatic Fever under Modified Jones Criteria?
?
**Evidence of preceding GAS infection** (ASO/anti-DNase B title or positive throat culture) PLUS:
- **2 Major criteria**, OR
- **1 Major + 2 Minor criteria**.
*(Exceptions: Sydenham chorea or indolent carditis alone need no streptococcal proof).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q8408265 -->

What are the 3 MAJOR criteria of the Modified Duke's Criteria (2023) for Infective Endocarditis?
?
1. **Microbiologic:** Typical organism from >=2 separate blood culture sets (S. aureus, Viridans group, E. faecalis, CoNS, HACEK).
2. **Imaging:** Echo/CT/PET-CT showing Vegetation, Abscess, Valve Perforation/Aneurysm, or new Prosthetic Dehiscence.
3. **Surgical:** Direct inspection evidence during cardiac surgery.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1988892 -->

What are the MINOR criteria of the Modified Duke's Criteria for Infective Endocarditis?
?
1. **Predisposition:** Previous IE, prosthetic valve, CHD, IVDU.
2. **Fever:** > 38.0 °C.
3. **Vascular phenomena:** Major emboli, septic infarcts, Janeway lesions (painless).
4. **Immunologic phenomena:** Osler nodes (painful), Roth spots, Glomerulonephritis, Rheumatoid Factor.
5. **Microbiologic evidence:** Positive blood culture not meeting major criteria.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1988892 -->

What are the requirements for a DEFINITE diagnosis of Infective Endocarditis under Duke Criteria?
?
- **2 Major criteria**, OR
- **1 Major + 3 Minor criteria**, OR
- **5 Minor criteria**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1988892 -->

What is the difference between Janeway lesions and Osler nodes in Infective Endocarditis?
?
- **Janeway lesions:** **Painless**, vascular/embolic macules on palms/soles.
- **Osler nodes:** **Painful**, immunologic nodules on fingers/toes.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1988892 -->

What JVP waveform and physical sign characterize Tricuspid Regurgitation?
?
**Giant v waves** (absent x, prominent y descent), **pulsatile liver**, and a pansystolic murmur louder on inspiration (**Carvallo sign**).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6193226 -->

What antibiotic regimen is used for Enterococcal Infective Endocarditis in a penicillin-allergic patient?
?
**Vancomycin + Gentamicin** (synergistic combination). Cephalosporins are intrinsically INACTIVE against enterococci.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4952304 -->

How should blood cultures be drawn for suspected Infective Endocarditis?
?
**2 to 3 separate sets** (aerobic + anaerobic) from **different sites** spaced over time, drawn **BEFORE starting antibiotics**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4914422 -->

What is the treatment for Late / Cardiovascular Syphilis (aortitis/aortic regurgitation)?
?
**Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM weekly for 3 weeks** (3 total doses).
*(Early syphilis requires only a single dose).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q2510508 -->

Which anticoagulant must be used for Atrial Fibrillation in moderate-to-severe Rheumatic Mitral Stenosis or Mechanical Valves?
?
**Warfarin** (Vitamin K antagonist). DOACs (Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban) are **contraindicated**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5632039 -->

Which cardiac lesion carries the LEAST risk of infective endocarditis?
?
A **small ostium secundum atrial septal defect (ASD)** (low-pressure, low-velocity shunt).
<!-- QBank ID: Q3586010 -->

What are the 4 HIGHEST-RISK cardiac indications for Infective Endocarditis antibiotic prophylaxis before dental work?
?
1. **Prosthetic heart valves** or valve repair material.
2. **Prior episode of Infective Endocarditis**.
3. **Unrepaired cyanotic CHD** or repaired CHD with residual defects / within 6 months.
4. **Cardiac transplant recipients** with valvulopathy.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7794661 -->

What is the standard antibiotic prophylaxis regimen before dental procedures in high-risk patients?
?
**Amoxicillin 2 grams PO** (or Ampicillin 2g IV/IM) 30 to 60 minutes before the procedure.
*(If penicillin-allergic: Azithromycin 500mg, Doxycycline 100mg, or Cephalexin 2g).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q7794661 -->

How does respiration affect right-sided vs left-sided cardiac murmurs?
?
- **Inspiration:** Increases **Right-sided** murmurs (**Carvallo sign**).
- **Expiration:** Increases **Left-sided** murmurs.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4742106 -->

How does sustained handgrip (increased afterload) affect cardiac murmurs?
?
- **INTENSIFIES:** Regurgitant murmurs (**Mitral Regurgitation**, **Aortic Regurgitation**, VSD).
- **SOFTENS:** **Aortic Stenosis** and **HOCM**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4742106 -->

What is the most common cause of myocarditis in developed countries, and what long-term structural change can it lead to?
?
**Viral infection** (Coxsackie B, Parvovirus B19, HHV-6); can progress to **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q6466740 -->

Is diphtheritic myocarditis caused by bacterial invasion or exotoxin?
?
It is caused by the **diphtheria exotoxin** (toxin-mediated, NOT bacteraemic).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6466740 -->

What is a Graham-Steell murmur?
?
A high-pitched early diastolic murmur at the upper left sternal border due to **functional pulmonary regurgitation** secondary to **pulmonary hypertension** (classically from severe mitral stenosis).
<!-- QBank ID: Q5631662 -->

What treatment is required for Fungal Endocarditis?
?
**Liposomal Amphotericin B** (+ Flucytosine) AND **valve surgery** (oral fluconazole alone is INADEQUATE).
<!-- QBank ID: Q7595167 -->

Compare Marantic Endocarditis vs Libman-Sacks Endocarditis.
?
- **Marantic (NBTE):** Sterile fibrin-platelet vegetations in **advanced malignancy / cancer**.
- **Libman-Sacks:** Sterile verrucous vegetations on **BOTH sides of valve** in **SLE / Antiphospholipid Syndrome**.
*(Both are STERILE and require NO antibiotics).*

# 📋 S2 Splitting & Heart Sounds Criteria Breakdown

What causes a FIXED SPLIT S2 (unaffected by respiration)?
?
**Atrial Septal Defect (ASD)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1458429 -->

What causes a WIDE SPLIT S2 (widens on inspiration)?
?
Delayed pulmonary valve closure (e.g., **Right Bundle Branch Block / RBBB**, **Pulmonary Stenosis**).
<!-- QBank ID: Q1458429 -->

What causes a PARADOXICAL / REVERSED SPLIT S2 (splits on EXPIRATION, closes on INSPIRATION)?
?
Delayed aortic valve closure (e.g., **Left Bundle Branch Block / LBBB**, **severe Aortic Stenosis**).
<!-- QBank ID: Q1458429 -->

What causes a LOUD A2 vs a LOUD P2?
?
- **Loud A2:** **Systemic Hypertension**.
- **Loud P2:** **Pulmonary Hypertension**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1458429 -->

What are the clinical findings of Mitral Regurgitation?
?
Blowing **pansystolic murmur at apex radiating to axilla**, **soft S1**, displaced apex beat, and **S3**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5001748 -->

What JVP and murmur findings define Tricuspid Stenosis?
?
**Large a wave**, **blunted y descent**, and a **mid-diastolic murmur** at lower left sternal border **increasing on inspiration**.

# 📋 Aortic Regurgitation (AR) Wide Pulse Pressure Signs Breakdown

What is a Corrigan / Water-Hammer Pulse?
?
A **rapidly swelling and abruptly collapsing pulse** (palpated at radial/brachial artery with arm elevated) due to wide pulse pressure in **Aortic Regurgitation**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2437236 -->

What is Traube Sign vs Duroziez Sign in Aortic Regurgitation?
?
- **Traube Sign:** **Pistol-shot booming sound** heard over uncompressed femoral arteries.
- **Duroziez Sign:** **To-and-fro murmur** heard over the femoral artery upon dual proximal/distal compression.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2437236 -->

What is Quincke Sign vs de Musset Sign in Aortic Regurgitation?
?
- **Quincke Sign:** Alternating flushing and blanching of **nailbed capillaries** upon gentle compression.
- **de Musset Sign:** **Head-bobbing** synchronous with each heartbeat.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2437236 -->

What is Hill Sign in Aortic Regurgitation?
?
**Popliteal Systolic BP exceeding Brachial Systolic BP by > 20 mmHg** (the most sensitive physical sign of chronic AR severity).
<!-- QBank ID: Q2437236 -->

What pulse type characterizes Aortic Stenosis (AS) vs Aortic Regurgitation (AR)?
?
- **Aortic Stenosis:** **Pulsus parvus et tardus** (small, slow-rising pulse).
- **Aortic Regurgitation:** **Collapsing / Water-hammer pulse** (bounding, rapid drop).

# 📋 Rheumatic Fever Secondary Prophylaxis Duration Criteria Breakdown

What is the duration of secondary penicillin prophylaxis for Rheumatic Fever WITHOUT carditis?
?
**5 years OR until age 21**, whichever is longer.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9890036 -->

What is the duration of secondary penicillin prophylaxis for Rheumatic Fever WITH carditis, but NO residual valvular disease?
?
**10 years OR until age 21**, whichever is longer.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9890036 -->

What is the duration of secondary penicillin prophylaxis for Rheumatic Fever WITH carditis AND residual valvular heart disease?
?
**10 years OR until age 40**, whichever is longer (often **lifelong** if severe).

# 📋 Constrictive Pericarditis vs Restrictive Cardiomyopathy Criteria Breakdown

What features strongly favor Constrictive Pericarditis over Restrictive Cardiomyopathy?
?
1. **Pericardial calcification / thickening** on CT/MRI.
2. **Pericardial knock**.
3. **Exaggerated respiratory variation** (ventricular interdependence / septal bounce).
4. **Dip-and-plateau (square-root sign)** on catheter pressure tracing.
*(Operative cure: Pericardiectomy).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q9290908 -->

What features strongly favor Restrictive Cardiomyopathy over Constrictive Pericarditis?
?
1. **Marked biatrial enlargement**.
2. **Thick ventricular myocardium** (e.g., Amyloidosis).
3. **Very high BNP / NT-proBNP levels**.
4. **High RV systolic pressure (>50 mmHg)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9290908 -->

What murmur combination represents combined Rheumatic Aortic & Mitral Regurgitation?
?
An **early diastolic murmur** at left sternal border (AR) + an **apical pansystolic murmur radiating to axilla** (MR).

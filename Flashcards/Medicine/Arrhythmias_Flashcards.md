# 📋 Electrophysiological Mechanisms Criteria Breakdown

What is the most common mechanism responsible for ~80% of tachyarrhythmias?
?
**Re-entry** (requires 2 pathways with different conduction speeds and refractory periods).
<!-- QBank ID: Q7600922 -->

What mechanism causes Torsades de Pointes in Long QT Syndrome?
?
Triggered activity from **Early Afterdepolarizations (EADs)** during phase 3/4 repolarization.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7600922 -->

What mechanism drives Digoxin toxicity & catecholaminergic arrhythmias?
?
Triggered activity from **Delayed Afterdepolarizations (DADs)** due to intracellular calcium overload.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7600922 -->

# 📋 Amiodarone Organ Toxicity Criteria Breakdown

What are the 5 major organ toxicities of Amiodarone?
?
1. **Lungs:** Pulmonary fibrosis / interstitial pneumonitis.
2. **Thyroid:** Hypothyroidism (AIH) or Hyperthyroidism (AIT) due to high iodine content.
3. **Eyes:** Corneal microdeposits (whorl keratopathy) & optic neuropathy.
4. **Skin:** Blue-grey slate skin discoloration & photosensitivity.
5. **Liver:** Transaminitis, hepatitis, cirrhosis.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3795885 -->

Does Amiodarone cause gynecomastia?
?
**No**. (Gynecomastia is associated with Spironolactone, Digoxin, Ketoconazole, Cimetidine).
<!-- QBank ID: Q3795885 -->

# 📋 ACLS Tachycardia Management Algorithm Criteria Breakdown

What is the immediate treatment for ANY tachyarrhythmia with HAEMODYNAMIC INSTABILITY (SBP < 90, shock, altered mental status, chest pain, acute HF)?
?
**Immediate Synchronized DC Cardioversion**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7055840 -->

Why must DC Cardioversion be SYNCHRONIZED to the R-wave in tachyarrhythmias with a pulse?
?
To prevent delivering a shock during the vulnerable T-wave (R-on-T phenomenon), which can trigger **Ventricular Fibrillation**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7055840 -->

What is the step-by-step management of STABLE Regular Narrow-Complex SVT?
?
1. **Vagal maneuvers** (Modified Valsalva or Carotid sinus massage).
2. **IV Adenosine** (6 mg rapid IV push $\rightarrow$ 12 mg $\rightarrow$ 12 mg).
3. **IV Verapamil / Diltiazem** or **Beta-blockers**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2070467 -->

What is the first-line drug for STABLE Monomorphic Wide-Complex Tachycardia (Ventricular Tachycardia)?
?
**IV Amiodarone** (150 mg bolus, then 1 mg/min infusion) or IV Procainamide.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3340351 -->

Why are Verapamil and Diltiazem strictly CONTRAINDICATED in Wide-Complex Tachycardia / VT?
?
They cause severe vasodilation and negative inotropy, leading to **cardiovascular collapse and Ventricular Fibrillation**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3340351 -->

What is the first-line treatment for Torsades de Pointes (Polymorphic VT)?
?
**IV Magnesium Sulphate (1 to 2 g IV)**, given **irrespective of serum magnesium level**, plus withdrawing QT-prolonging drugs.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3331749 -->

Why is Amiodarone CONTRAINDICATED in Torsades de Pointes?
?
Amiodarone is a Class III antiarrhythmic that **prolongs the QT interval**, worsening Torsades de Pointes.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3331749 -->

What 4 features define Atrial Fibrillation on examination and ECG?
?
1. **Irregularly irregular pulse**.
2. **Pulse deficit** (Apex rate > Radial pulse rate).
3. **Absent a wave** in JVP.
4. **Absent P waves** (replaced by f waves) on ECG.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4115126 -->

# 📋 CHA2DS2-VASc vs HAS-BLED Criteria Breakdown

What score is used to decide whether to start anticoagulation for stroke prevention in non-valvular AF?
?
**CHA2DS2-VASc score** (Anticoagulate if score $\ge 2$ in men, $\ge 3$ in women).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9302980 -->

What is the purpose of the HAS-BLED score in Atrial Fibrillation?
?
To identify and address **modifiable bleeding risk factors** (it is NOT used to withhold anticoagulation).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9302980 -->

What are the components of the CHA2DS2-VASc score and their point values?
?
- **C** - Congestive Heart Failure (1)
- **H** - Hypertension (1)
- **A2** - Age $\ge 75$ years (**2**)
- **D** - Diabetes Mellitus (1)
- **S2** - Stroke / TIA / Thromboembolism (**2**)
- **V** - Vascular Disease (1)
- **A** - Age 65-74 years (1)
- **Sc** - Sex Category Female (1)
<!-- QBank ID: Q9302980 -->

Which anticoagulants are preferred in Non-Valvular AF vs Valvular AF?
?
- **Non-Valvular AF:** **DOACs** (Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban) are preferred over Warfarin.
- **Valvular AF (mod-to-severe MS / mechanical valve):** **Warfarin** is MANDATORY.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9302980 -->

# 📋 Pre-Excited AF (WPW + AF) Drug Contraindications Mnemonic (ABCD)

What 4 AV-nodal blocking drugs are CONTRAINDICATED in Wolff-Parkinson-White with Atrial Fibrillation?
?
- **A**denosine
- **B**eta-blockers
- **C**alcium channel blockers (Verapamil / Diltiazem)
- **D**igoxin
*(Mnemonic: **ABCD**; blocking AV node forces all impulses down accessory pathway $\rightarrow$ Ventricular Fibrillation).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q8051976 -->

What are the drugs of choice for Pre-Excited AF (WPW + AF)?
?
**Procainamide** or **Ibutilide** (or DC Cardioversion if hemodynamically unstable).
<!-- QBank ID: Q8051976 -->

# 📋 AV Block Classification & Pacemaker Criteria Breakdown

What JVP sign is classic for Complete (Third-Degree) Heart Block?
?
**Intermittent cannon a waves** (right atrium contracting against a closed tricuspid valve).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9675335 -->

Which AV heart blocks require a Permanent Pacemaker (PPM) even if ASYMPTOMATIC?
?
- **Mobitz Type II**
- **Complete (Third-Degree) Heart Block**
<!-- QBank ID: Q9675335 -->

Why does Atropine fail in Mobitz Type II and Complete Infranodal Heart Block?
?
Atropine acts on the **AV node** (nodal level) and has no effect on infranodal (His-Purkinje) conduction.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7265126 -->

What is the immediate next step for symptomatic infranodal block when Atropine fails?
?
**Transcutaneous or Transvenous Pacing** (plus Adrenaline/Dopamine infusion as a bridge).
<!-- QBank ID: Q7265126 -->

What is the single most common indication for a permanent pacemaker (PPM)?
?
**Symptomatic Sinus Node Dysfunction** (Sick Sinus Syndrome).
<!-- QBank ID: Q8268131 -->

Why is a Dual-Chamber (DDD) Pacemaker preferred over Single-Chamber Ventricular (VVI) Pacing when sinus rhythm is present?
?
To preserve **AV synchrony / atrial transport ("atrial kick")** and avoid **pacemaker syndrome**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8268131 -->

# 📋 ACLS Cardiac Arrest Shockable vs Non-Shockable Criteria

What are the SHOCKABLE rhythms in Cardiac Arrest, and how are they treated?
?
- **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)** & **Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT)**.
- **Immediate UNSYNCHRONIZED Defibrillation** + CPR + Adrenaline every 3-5 min (after 2nd shock) + Amiodarone 300 mg (after 3rd shock).
<!-- QBank ID: Q4247278 -->

What are the NON-SHOCKABLE rhythms in Cardiac Arrest?
?
- **Asystole** & **Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA)**.
*(NEVER shocked; managed with immediate CPR + Adrenaline 1 mg IV ASAP every 3-5 min).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q4247278 -->

# 📋 Hyperkalemia ECG Stages & Treatment Stepwise Criteria

What is the sequential ECG progression of Hyperkalemia?
?
1. **$K^+ 5.5 - 6.5$:** Tall, narrow-based, peaked (tented) T waves.
2. **$K^+ 6.5 - 7.0$:** PR prolongation, QRS widening, P wave flattening/loss.
3. **$K^+ > 7.0$:** **Sine-wave pattern** $\rightarrow$ VF / Asystole.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1739798 -->

What is the 3-step management algorithm for Hyperkalemia?
?
1. **Step 1 (Membrane Stabilization / Cardioprotection):** **IV Calcium Gluconate (10%)** (does NOT lower potassium!).
2. **Step 2 (Shift $K^+$ intracellularly):** **IV Insulin + Dextrose**, Nebulized Salbutamol, IV Sodium Bicarbonate.
3. **Step 3 (Eliminate $K^+$ from body):** **Hemodialysis**, Furosemide, Potassium Binders.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1739798 -->

# 📋 AF Rate Control Algorithm by LVEF Criteria (ESC 2024)

What rate control drugs are approved in AF with LVEF > 40% vs LVEF $\le$ 40% (HFrEF)?
?
- **LVEF > 40%:** Beta-blocker OR Non-DHP CCB (Verapamil / Diltiazem) OR Digoxin.
- **LVEF $\le$ 40% (HFrEF):** **Beta-blocker AND/OR Digoxin ONLY**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5651744 -->

Why are Verapamil and Diltiazem CONTRAINDICATED in AF with HFrEF / Heart Failure?
?
They exert **potent negative inotropic effects**, precipitating acute heart failure decompensation and shock.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5651744 -->

# 📋 Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Triad & TCA Overdose Criteria

What is the classic resting ECG triad of Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome?
?
1. **Short PR interval (<120 ms)**
2. **Delta wave** (slurred upstroke of QRS)
3. **Wide QRS complex (>120 ms)**
<!-- QBank ID: Q1616076 -->

What are the 2 classic ECG findings of Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Overdose?
?
1. **QRS Widening (>100 ms)** (predicts seizures/arrhythmia).
2. **Prominent terminal R wave in lead aVR ($R_{\text{aVR}} > 3\text{ mm}$)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3345848 -->

What is the first-line treatment for TCA Overdose with QRS widening or hypotension?
?
**IV Sodium Bicarbonate (1-2 mEq/kg)** (alkalizes serum and provides sodium load to unblock Na+ channels).
<!-- QBank ID: Q3345848 -->

# 📋 Antihistamines QT Prolongation Risk Criteria

Which older antihistamines cause severe hERG channel blockade, QT prolongation, and Torsades when combined with CYP3A4 inhibitors (macrolides/azoles)?
?
**Astemizole** and **Terfenadine** (both withdrawn).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6853724 -->

Which antihistamines are CARDIAC-SAFE (no hERG channel QT prolongation risk)?
?
**Fexofenadine**, **Cetirizine**, and **Loratadine**.

# 📋 Jugular Venous Pulse (JVP) Abnormality Master Criteria Breakdown

What cause produces an ABSENT a wave in JVP?
?
**Atrial Fibrillation** (loss of organized atrial contraction).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9100324 -->

What cause produces a LARGE / PROMINENT a wave in JVP?
?
**Tricuspid Stenosis** (or Pulmonary Hypertension / Pulmonary Stenosis).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9100324 -->

What cause produces INTERMITTENT CANNON a waves in JVP?
?
**Complete (3rd Degree) AV Block** or **Ventricular Tachycardia** (AV dissociation).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9100324 -->

What cause produces a GIANT v wave in JVP?
?
**Tricuspid Regurgitation**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9100324 -->

What condition produces a STEEP / PROMINENT y descent in JVP?
?
**Constrictive Pericarditis** (Friedreich sign).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9100324 -->

# 📋 Antiarrhythmic Drug Toxicity Specifics Criteria

Which Class IA antiarrhythmic is famous for causing Drug-Induced Lupus Erythematosus (DILE)?
?
**Procainamide**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8083651 -->

What is the primary toxicity of Lidocaine (Class IB)?
?
**Central Nervous System toxicity** (perioral numbness, confusion, tinnitus, seizures).
<!-- QBank ID: Q8083651 -->

Why are Class IC antiarrhythmics (Flecainide, Propafenone) CONTRAINDICATED in structural/ischemic heart disease?
?
They are highly **pro-arrhythmic** in ischemic myocardium (CAST trial).
<!-- QBank ID: Q8083651 -->

What ECG wave is characteristic of Hypokalemia vs Hypothermia?
?
- **Hypokalemia:** **U waves**, ST depression, flat T waves.
- **Hypothermia:** **Osborn (J) waves** (convex bump at J-point).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9474851 -->

# 📋 Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT) Criteria Breakdown

What defines Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT) on ECG?
?
**$\ge 3$ different P-wave shapes** in the same lead with irregular PP/PR intervals and rate > 100 bpm.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5418343 -->

What is the first-line treatment for Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT)?
?
**Treat the underlying lung disease and hypoxia** (Oxygen, bronchodilators, electrolytes). *(Cardioversion is CONTRAINDICATED).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q5418343 -->

# 📋 Brugada Syndrome & Long-QT Syndrome Criteria Breakdown

What is the genetic mutation and ECG pattern of Brugada Syndrome?
?
- **Gene:** Loss-of-function mutation in **SCN5A** ($\text{Na}^+$ channel).
- **ECG:** **Coved ST elevation ($\ge 2\text{ mm}$)** with T wave inversion in **V1 to V3**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5094120 -->

What is the ONLY definitive treatment to prevent Sudden Cardiac Death in Brugada Syndrome?
?
**Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD)**. *(Beta-blockers do NOT protect).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q5094120 -->

What are the triggers for LQT1 vs LQT2 vs LQT3 in Congenital Long-QT Syndrome?
?
- **LQT1 (KCNQ1):** **Exercise / Swimming**.
- **LQT2 (KCNH2):** **Emotional stress / Sudden loud noises**.
- **LQT3 (SCN5A):** **Sleep / Rest**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7081414 -->

What is the first-line long-term medical management for Congenital LQTS?
?
**Beta-blockers** (Nadolol/Propranolol) + strict avoidance of QT-prolonging drugs.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7081414 -->

# 📋 ECG Cardiac Axis & His-Bundle Electrogram Criteria Breakdown

How do Lead I and Lead aVF determine Cardiac Axis?
?
- **Lead I (+) & aVF (+):** **Normal Axis** ($-30^\circ$ to $+90^\circ$).
- **Lead I (-) & aVF (+):** **Right Axis Deviation** (RVH, COPD, Secundum ASD).
- **Lead I (+) & aVF (-):** **Left Axis Deviation** (LAFB, Primum ASD, Inferior MI).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9868728 -->

What axis deviation is seen in Ostium Secundum ASD vs Ostium Primum ASD?
?
- **Ostium Secundum ASD:** **Right Axis Deviation (RAD)**.
- **Ostium Primum ASD:** **Left Axis Deviation (LAD)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9868728 -->

What do the AH interval and HV interval measure on a His-Bundle Electrogram?
?
- **AH Interval:** Conduction across the **AV node** (normal < 130 ms).
- **HV Interval:** Conduction from **His bundle through bundle branches to ventricles** (normal $\le 55\text{ ms}$).
<!-- QBank ID: Q4121873 -->

What is the classic ECG feature and ventricular rate of Atrial Flutter with 2:1 block?
?
**Sawtooth F waves** at atrial rate $\sim 300\text{ bpm}$ $\rightarrow$ **ventricular rate $\sim 150\text{ bpm}$**.
*(Ablation target: Cavotricuspid Isthmus / CTI).*

# 📋 AF Cardioversion Anticoagulation Timing Rules Criteria

How long MUST anticoagulation be continued AFTER successful cardioversion of Atrial Fibrillation?
?
At least **4 weeks** for ALL patients (due to **atrial stunning** and transient thromboembolic risk).
<!-- QBank ID: Q1192958 -->

What are the pre-cardioversion anticoagulation requirements if AF has lasted > 24 hours?
?
**3 weeks of therapeutic anticoagulation** BEFORE elective cardioversion OR a **Transesophageal Echo (TEE)** to rule out LAA clot.
<!-- QBank ID: Q1192958 -->

# 📋 Ventricular Ectopy & Warfarin Drug Interactions Criteria

What is Ventricular Bigeminy vs Ventricular Tachycardia?
?
- **Bigeminy:** Regular 1:1 alternation of a sinus beat with a premature wide QRS (PVC).
- **VT:** $\ge 3$ consecutive premature wide QRS complexes in a row at > 100 bpm.
<!-- QBank ID: Q5012733 -->

Which macrolides inhibit CYP450 and spike Warfarin INR vs which macrolide is safer?
?
- **Spike INR / High Bleeding Risk:** **Clarithromycin** & **Erythromycin**.
- **Safer Macrolide:** **Azithromycin** (minimal CYP inhibition).
<!-- QBank ID: Q9616125 -->

# 📋 Atrial Enlargement P-Wave ECG Criteria Breakdown

What defines P pulmonale vs P mitrale on ECG?
?

- **P mitrale (LAE):** **Broad, bifid / notched P wave $\ge 120\text{ ms}$** in lead II (Mitral stenosis/regurgitation).

# 📋 Adenosine Pharmacology & Contraindications Criteria Breakdown

What receptor does Adenosine activate and why is it CONTRAINDICATED in Asthma?
?
Activates **A1 purinergic receptors** in AV node; contraindicated in Asthma because it activates **A2B/A3 receptors causing bronchospasm**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q4897348 -->

Which drugs antagonize vs potentiate Adenosine?
?
- **Antagonize (require higher dose):** **Theophylline** and **Caffeine**.
- **Potentiate (require lower dose):** **Dipyridamole** (blocks reuptake).
<!-- QBank ID: Q4897348 -->

# 📋 Pacemaker Complications Criteria Breakdown

What type of pacemaker causes Pacemaker-Mediated Tachycardia (PMT) vs Pacemaker Syndrome?
?
- **PMT (Endless loop tachycardia):** Exclusive to **Dual-Chamber (DDD) Pacemakers**.
- **Pacemaker Syndrome (Loss of AV synchrony):** Typical of **Single-Chamber Ventricular (VVI) Pacing** during sinus rhythm.

# 📋 Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) Criteria Breakdown

What are the Sokolow-Lyon and Cornell voltage criteria for Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)?
?
- **Sokolow-Lyon:** $S_{\text{V1}} + R_{\text{V5 or V6}} \ge 35\text{ mm}$ (or $R_{\text{aVL}} > 11\text{ mm}$).
- **Cornell Voltage:** $S_{\text{V3}} + R_{\text{aVL}} > 28\text{ mm}$ (men) / $> 20\text{ mm}$ (women).
<!-- QBank ID: Q2532372 -->

What is an LV Strain Pattern on ECG?
?
**Down-sloping ST depression & T-wave inversion** in the lateral leads (I, aVL, V5, V6).
<!-- QBank ID: Q2532372 -->

# 📋 Second-Degree AV Block Subtype Criteria Breakdown

How do Mobitz Type I (Wenckebach) and Mobitz Type II differ on ECG?
?
- **Mobitz I (Wenckebach):** **Progressive PR lengthening** until a QRS is dropped (AV node; benign).
- **Mobitz II:** **Constant PR interval** with sudden non-conducted P wave / dropped QRS (Infranodal; needs Pacemaker).

# 📋 Complete Heart Block & Escape Rhythms Criteria Breakdown

What defines Complete (Third-Degree) Heart Block on ECG?
?
**Complete AV Dissociation** (P waves and QRS complexes beat independently at regular, separate rates).
<!-- QBank ID: Q4865472 -->

What are the heart rates and QRS widths of Junctional vs Ventricular Escape Rhythms?
?
- **Junctional Escape:** Narrow QRS, rate **40 to 60 bpm**.
- **Ventricular Escape:** Wide QRS, rate **< 40 bpm** (causes syncope / Stokes-Adams attacks).

# 📋 Bundle Branch Block Criteria & Mnemonics Breakdown

What are the ECG features and mnemonics for RBBB vs LBBB?
?
- **RBBB (MARROW):** **M** in V1 ($RSR'$ rabbit ears) & **W** in V6 (wide slurred S).
- **LBBB (WILLIAM):** **W** in V1 (broad QS) & **M** in V6 (broad notched R).
*(QRS $\ge 120\text{ ms}$ for both).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q7790367 -->

# 📋 Supraventricular Tachycardias Differential Criteria Breakdown

How do Atrial Fibrillation, Atrial Flutter, and MAT differ on ECG?
?
- **Atrial Fibrillation:** **Absent P waves** + **Irregularly irregular** rhythm + chaotic baseline.
- **Atrial Flutter:** **Sawtooth F waves** ($\sim 300\text{ bpm}$) + **Regular** (often 2:1 block $\rightarrow 150\text{ bpm}$).
- **Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT):** **$\ge 3$ P wave shapes** + **Irregularly irregular** rhythm (COPD).
<!-- QBank ID: Q7835810 -->

# Upper Limb Flashcards

What is the most common site of clavicle fracture, and why?
?
The **middle 1/3rd**, because it is the weak transition point between its two opposing curves and lacks muscle protection.
<!-- QBank ID: Q3234477 -->

Where does ~75% of breast lymph (especially from outer quadrants) drain first?
?
Into the **anterior (pectoral) axillary lymph nodes**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8049978 -->

What structure passes around the lower border of pectoralis major into the axilla?
?
The **axillary tail of Spence** (upper outer part of the breast).
<!-- QBank ID: Q6275978 -->

What causes skin dimpling/puckering in breast carcinoma?
?
Tethering and shortening of the **suspensory ligaments of Cooper**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9696980 -->

What causes nipple retraction in breast carcinoma?
?
Tension / pull along the **lactiferous ducts**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q9696980 -->

Where does the spine of the scapula end laterally?
?
It expands to form the **acromion process** (NOT the coracoid process).
<!-- QBank ID: Q8100805 -->

Which scapular process is palpable from the front in the infraclavicular fossa?
?
The **coracoid process**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q8100805 -->

Which 4 structures pierce the clavipectoral fascia?
?
1. **Cephalic vein** (going in)
2. **Thoracoacromial artery branches** (going out)
3. **Lateral pectoral nerve** (going out)
4. **Lymphatics** (from breast to apical nodes)
*(Musculocutaneous nerve does NOT).*
<!-- QBank ID: Q9363634 -->

Which muscle divides the axillary artery into 3 parts, and how many branches arise from each part?
?
**Pectoralis minor**.
- 1st part: 1 branch (Superior thoracic)
- 2nd part: 2 branches (Thoracoacromial, Lateral thoracic)
- 3rd part: 3 branches (Subscapular, Ant & Post circumflex humeral)
<!-- QBank ID: Q3231691 -->

Is the suprascapular artery a branch of the axillary artery?
?
**No**, it is a branch of the **thyrocervical trunk** (from the subclavian artery).
<!-- QBank ID: Q5196120 -->

Paralysis of which muscle and nerve causes winging of the scapula when pushing against a wall?
?
**Serratus anterior** muscle; **Long thoracic nerve of Bell (C5-C7)**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q2842018 -->

What nerve (arising from C5) supplies the rhomboids and levator scapulae?
?
**Dorsal scapular nerve**.
<!-- QBank ID: Q7278082 -->

What nerve injury causes inability to make the "OK" sign following a supracondylar fracture of the humerus?
?
**Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN)** (deep branch of median nerve). It supplies **flexor pollicis longus (FPL)** (thumb IP flexion) and **flexor digitorum profundus (FDP, lateral half)** (index finger DIP flexion).

What nerve roots are damaged in Erb's palsy, and what is the characteristic limb deformity?
?
**C5 and C6** (upper trunk brachial plexus). Deformity is **"Waiter's tip" (Policeman's tip)**: shoulder adducted & internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated.

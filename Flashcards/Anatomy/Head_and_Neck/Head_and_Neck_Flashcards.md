Which cranial nerve represents a direct extension of the CNS and passes through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone?
?

What classic triad characterizes Horner's syndrome, and what structure is disrupted when second-order sympathetic neurons are affected?
?
The triad of **ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis** caused by disruption of the **cervical sympathetic chain** (second-order sympathetic pathway from C8-T2 to the superior cervical ganglion).

Which foramen at the base of the skull transmits the mandibular nerve (V3), supplying the muscles of mastication derived from the 1st pharyngeal arch?
?
**Foramen ovale** (transmits the Mandibular nerve V3, which supplies 1st pharyngeal arch derivatives including muscles of mastication).

Which muscle is primarily responsible for opening (depressing) the jaw, and what are its origins, insertion, and nerve supply?
?
**Lateral Pterygoid muscle**.
- **Action:** Depresses (opens) and protracts the mandible.
- **Origin:** Upper head: infratemporal surface of greater wing of sphenoid; Lower head: **lateral surface of lateral pterygoid plate**.
- **Insertion:** Pterygoid fovea (neck of mandible) & TMJ articular disc.
- **Nerve Supply:** Mandibular nerve (V3 - anterior division).

Which muscle of the soft palate is innervated by the mandibular nerve (V3) rather than the pharyngeal plexus (CN X), making it an exception among palatal muscles in dysarthria and dysphagia involvement?
?
**Tensor veli palatini**.
- **Nerve Supply:** Mandibular nerve (V3 via nerve to medial pterygoid).
- **Action:** Tenses soft palate & opens Eustachian tube.
- **Contrast:** Levator veli palatini, pharyngeal constrictors, and laryngeal muscles are innervated by **CN X (Vagus nerve)** via pharyngeal plexus, causing combined dysphagia & dysarthria when paralyzed.

Which bone articulates with the superior articular facets of the first cervical vertebra (Atlas / C1), and what joint is formed?
?
The **Occipital bone** (specifically its occipital condyles), forming the **atlanto-occipital joint** (enables head nodding / "yes" movement).

Key features of Atlas (C1):
- **Ring-shaped:** Lacks a vertebral body and spinous process.
- **Superior articular facets:** Elongated/oval; articulate with Occipital bone.
- **Inferior articular facets:** Rounded; articulate with Axis (C2).

# General Anatomy Flashcards

What type of epiphysis is formed by the repeated pull of a tendon (e.g., greater trochanter)?
?
**Traction epiphysis**
Takeaway: Tendon pull builds a traction epiphysis; joint loading builds a pressure epiphysis.

What type of epiphysis lies at weight-transmitting articular ends (e.g., femoral head)?
?
**Pressure epiphysis**

What type of epiphysis is the coracoid process?
?
**Atavistic epiphysis** (independent in evolutionary ancestors, now fused).

What type of epiphysis is the head of the first metacarpal?
?
**Aberrant epiphysis** (normally absent).

Which dermatome corresponds exactly to the umbilicus?
?
**T10**

Which dermatomes correspond to the nipple line, xiphoid process, and groin respectively?
?
- **Nipple:** T4
- **Xiphoid process:** T7
- **Groin:** L1

Which anatomical plane divides the body into anterior (front) and posterior (back) parts?
?
**Coronal (frontal) plane**

Which anatomical plane divides the body into top and bottom parts?
?
**Transverse plane**

What is the preferred standard operating position for perineal, obstetric, and genitourinary procedures?
?
**Lithotomy position** (lays the patient on the back with hips and knees flexed).

Which fontanelle is the last to close, and when does this normally occur?
?
**Anterior fontanelle (bregma)**; closes between **12 and 24 months**.

When does the posterior fontanelle normally close?
?
By about **3 months**.

What is Hilton's law regarding joint innervation?
?

<!-- QBank ID: Q4171028 -->

Name the four structural types of epiphyses with their classic anatomical examples.
?
- **Pressure Epiphysis:** Articular, weight-bearing ends (e.g., Head of femur, Head of humerus, Lower end of radius).
- **Traction Epiphysis:** Non-articular, tendon attachment pulled by muscle (e.g., Greater/Lesser trochanters of femur, Tubercle of humerus).
- **Atavistic Epiphysis:** Phylogenetically independent bone annexed to neighbour (e.g., Coracoid process of scapula).
- **Aberrant Epiphysis:** Epiphysis in unusual location (e.g., Head of 1st metacarpal).

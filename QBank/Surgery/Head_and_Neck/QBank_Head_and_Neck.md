## Q1. Carotid Body Tumor (Chemodectoma) Workup & Features
**Question ID:** Q9836796
**Question:** 50/M presents with neck swelling as shown. Examination reveals firm swelling, doesn't move on deglutition, elicits transmitted pulsations with lateral mobility. Which of these investigations are desirable?
1. USG neck
2. CT neck angiogram
3. FNAC
4. PET SCAN

- A) 1 and 2
- B) 1, 2 and 4
- C) 2 only
- D) 2 and 4

**Answer:** A) 1 and 2

**Explanation:**
- **Clinical Diagnosis:** A firm neck mass at the carotid triangle that is mobile side-to-side but fixed vertically (**Fountain / Fontaine sign**) with transmitted/expansile pulsations is classic for a **Carotid Body Tumor (Paraganglioma / Chemodectoma)**.
- **Desirable Workup:**
  1. **USG Neck:** Initial non-invasive imaging to assess vascularity and relationship to carotid bifurcation.
  2. **CT Neck Angiogram (CTA) / Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA):** **Investigation of Choice (IOC)**. Classically demonstrates the **Lyre Sign** (splaying of the internal and external carotid arteries at the bifurcation).
- **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION:**
  - **FNAC / Biopsy:** CONTRAINDICATED in vascular neck masses due to high risk of severe bleeding and expansive hematoma formation!

### 📋 Carotid Body Tumor (Chemodectoma) Master Summary Table:

| Parameter | High-Yield Clinical / Surgical Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Origin Cell Type** | Neural crest cells (paraganglia / non-chromaffin chemoreceptors) |
| **Risk Factor** | Chronic hypoxia / **High-altitude living** |
| **Classic Physical Sign** | **Fontaine / Fountain Sign** (mobile laterally, fixed vertically due to carotid attachment) |
| **Deglutition / Tongue Protrusion** | Does NOT move with deglutition (vs Thyroid) or tongue protrusion (vs Thyroglossal cyst) |
| **Investigation of Choice (IOC)** | **CT Angiography / DSA** (shows **Lyre Sign** — splaying of ICA & ECA) |
| **Contraindicated Investigation** | **FNAC / Biopsy** (risk of massive hemorrhage) |
| **Most Commonly Injured Nerve** | **Hypoglossal Nerve (CN XII)** during surgical excision |

**Take Home Message:** Vascular neck masses are best evaluated with ultrasound and CT angiogram to determine vascular involvement and anatomy. FNAC is generally avoided in vascular lesions due to bleeding risk.

# Pediatric Hematology and Oncology QBank

## Q1. Initial Treatment for Pediatric Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)
**Question ID:** Q6114677
**Question:** A young boy presents with a platelet count of 10,000/µL. Bone marrow aspirate reveals normal cellularity with increased number of megakaryocytes. What is the most appropriate initial treatment for this condition? (INICET MAY 2025)
- A) Methotrexate
- B) Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)
- C) Bone Marrow Transplant
- D) Aspirin

**Answer:** B) Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)

**Explanation:**
This clinical presentation is consistent with immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP). The key features include severe thrombocytopenia (platelet count 10,000/µL), normal bone marrow cellularity with increased megakaryocytes, indicating peripheral platelet destruction rather than production failure. In patients with life-threatening thrombocytopenia or severe bleeding in ITP, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is the first-line treatment. IVIG works by blocking or saturating macrophage Fc receptors, providing faster response (24-48 hours) compared to corticosteroids. In non-bleeding patients without urgent symptoms, corticosteroids would be the first-line treatment, but given the severely low platelet count, IVIG is more appropriate for immediate management.

**Other Options:**
- **Option A (Methotrexate):** This immunosuppressive agent is not used in the initial treatment of ITP. It may be considered as a second-line or third-line agent in refractory cases, but it is not appropriate for initial management, especially in a young patient with severe thrombocytopenia.
- **Option C (Bone Marrow Transplant):** This is not indicated in ITP since the condition involves peripheral destruction of platelets rather than bone marrow failure. The bone marrow is functioning normally with increased megakaryocyte production, making transplantation unnecessary and inappropriate.
- **Option D (Aspirin):** This would be contraindicated in a patient with severe thrombocytopenia as it would further impair platelet function and significantly increase bleeding risk. Aspirin inhibits platelet aggregation, which would be dangerous in someone with already critically low platelet counts.

**Take Home Message:** In pediatric ITP with severe thrombocytopenia (<10,000/µL) and bleeding risk, IVIG provides rapid response by blocking macrophage Fc receptors and is the preferred initial treatment.

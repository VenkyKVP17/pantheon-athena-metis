# Basic Obstetrics, Placenta & Fetal Physiology QBank

## Q1. Fetal Circulation Shunts and Oxygenation Pathways
**Question ID:** Q4183116
**Question:** A medical student is asked to trace the path of oxygenated blood in a healthy term fetus before birth. All of the following statements about the fetal circulation are correct EXCEPT:
- A) The umbilical vein carries the most highly oxygenated blood found anywhere in the fetus.
- B) The ductus venosus lets umbilical venous blood bypass the hepatic sinusoids and reach the inferior vena cava.
- C) Blood returning to the placenta for reoxygenation leaves through the paired umbilical arteries arising from the internal iliac vessels.
- D) The ductus arteriosus directs the greater part of right ventricular output into the pulmonary trunk, so that the lungs receive most of the cardiac output.

**Answer:** D) The ductus arteriosus directs the greater part of right ventricular output into the pulmonary trunk, so that the lungs receive most of the cardiac output.

**Explanation:**
- The fetal lungs are collapsed, fluid-filled, and non-functional, with very high pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR). 
- The **ductus arteriosus** connects the pulmonary trunk to the descending aorta. It shunts **~90% of right ventricular output AWAY from the lungs into the aorta**, so the lungs receive only 8 to 10% of the right ventricular output.
- **Oxygenation Gradient:**
  - **Umbilical Vein:** Carries the most highly oxygenated blood (~80% O2 sat) from placenta to fetus.
  - **Ductus Venosus:** Shunts well-oxygenated umbilical venous blood past the liver directly into the IVC.
  - **Foramen Ovale:** Preferentially streams IVC oxygenated blood from RA $\rightarrow$ LA $\rightarrow$ LV $\rightarrow$ Ascending Aorta (supplying heart and brain).
  - **Umbilical Arteries (2):** Arise from internal iliac (hypogastric) arteries and carry deoxygenated blood back to the placenta.

**Take Home Message:** Ductus arteriosus shunts 90% of RV blood into the aorta away from lungs; umbilical vein carries highest O2 blood and umbilical arteries (2) return deoxygenated blood.

## Q2. Decidual Regions & Placental Cleavage Plane
**Question ID:** Q4096410
**Question:** A blastocyst has implanted and the endometrium has transformed into decidua. Regarding the decidua in pregnancy, which of the statements below are true?
1. The decidua basalis forms deep to the implanting blastocyst and becomes the maternal part of the placenta.
2. The plane along which the placenta separates in the third stage of labour passes through the spongy layer of the decidua.
3. The decidua capsularis eventually fuses with the decidua basalis and obliterates the endometrial cavity by the second trimester.
4. The decidua parietalis lines the implantation site and forms the maternal part of the placenta.

- A) 1, 2 and 3 only.
- B) 1 and 2 only.
- C) 2 and 4 only.
- D) 1, 2, 3 and 4.

**Answer:** B) 1 and 2 only.

**Explanation:**
- **Decidua Basalis:** Lies deep to conceptus, forms the MATERNAL portion of placenta (*chorion frondosum* forms fetal portion).
- **Decidua Capsularis:** Overlies conceptus. Fuses with **decidua parietalis (vera)** (NOT basalis) by 10–12 weeks to obliterate uterine cavity.
- **Decidua Parietalis (Vera):** Lines rest of uterine cavity away from implantation site.
- **Decidual Layers:**
  1. Superficial Compact Zone
  2. Middle Spongy Zone (loose, dilated glands $\rightarrow$ **natural cleavage plane** for placental separation in 3rd stage of labor)
  3. Thin Basal Zone (retained to regenerate endometrium).

**Take Home Message:** Decidua basalis forms maternal placenta; spongy zone is the cleavage plane for placental separation; capsularis fuses with parietalis.

## Q3. Gametogenesis: Spermatogenesis vs Oogenesis
**Question ID:** Q6165033
**Question:** A physiology tutor contrasts how sperm and eggs are made. Concerning spermatogenesis and oogenesis, identify the correct statements:
1. Spermatogenesis takes about 60 to 64 days, and each primary spermatocyte finally yields four spermatozoa.
2. The oogonial pool peaks at roughly seven million around the twentieth week of intrauterine life, then falls to about one to two million by birth.
3. Oogenesis, like spermatogenesis, produces four equal and functional gametes from each primary germ cell.
4. Spermatogenesis proceeds under testosterone alone and does not need pituitary gonadotrophins.

- A) 2 and 3 only.
- B) 1, 2 and 4 only.
- C) 1 and 2 only.
- D) 1, 2, 3 and 4.

**Answer:** C) 1 and 2 only.

**Explanation:**
- **Spermatogenesis:** 1 primary spermatocyte $\rightarrow$ 4 functional spermatozoa (~60–64 days). Requires LH (drives Leydig cells $\rightarrow$ testosterone) and FSH (acts on Sertoli cells).
- **Oogenesis Pool:** Peaks at **7 million oogonia at 20 weeks in utero** $\rightarrow$ drops to 1–2 million at birth $\rightarrow$ ~400,000 at puberty $\rightarrow$ ~100 at menopause.
- **Meiotic Yield:** Oogenesis produces **1 functional ovum + 3 polar bodies** (due to unequal cytoplasmic division), NOT 4 functional gametes.
- **Meiotic Arrest Points:**
  - **Arrest 1:** Prophase I (**Diplotene stage**) until puberty.
  - **Arrest 2:** **Metaphase II** at ovulation. Completed ONLY upon fertilization.

**Take Home Message:** Oogonia peak at 7M at 20w in utero; 1 primary oocyte yields 1 ovum + 3 polar bodies; primary oocyte arrests in diplotene of Prophase I, secondary oocyte arrests in Metaphase II.

# Mycology QBank

## Q1. Diagnostic Stains for Pneumocystis jirovecii in Immunocompromised Patients
**Question ID:** Q6374679
**Question:** A post-renal transplant patient presents with fever and breathlessness. Lung biopsy is done. Which of the following stains is least useful in identifying the causative organism? (INICET MAY 2025)
- A) Congo red
- B) Periodic Acid-Schiff
- C) Gomori Methenamine Silver
- D) Fontana-Masson

**Answer:** A) Congo red

**Explanation:**
- **Congo Red Stain:** Used specifically to detect **amyloid deposits**, which display characteristic **apple-green birefringence** under polarized light. It is **least useful** for identifying infectious organisms like *Pneumocystis jirovecii*.
- **Clinical Context:** In a post-renal transplant (immunocompromised) patient with fever and dyspnea, the most likely diagnosis is **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)**. Lung biopsy shows characteristic cysts of *Pneumocystis*.
- **Stains Used for Pneumocystis jirovecii / Opportunistic Fungi:**
  - **Gomori Methenamine Silver (GMS):** Considered the **gold standard** for staining fungal elements, including *Pneumocystis jirovecii* cysts (stains cyst walls black/dark brown).
  - **Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS):** Excellent stain for detecting the cyst wall of *Pneumocystis jirovecii* and fungal cell walls (magenta/pink).
  - **Fontana-Masson:** Useful for identifying argentophilic (silver-loving) organisms such as *Cryptococcus neoformans* and certain fungal elements, making it more useful than Congo red in an infectious context.

**Take Home Message:** Congo red stain detects amyloid deposits (apple-green birefringence) and is non-diagnostic for fungal organisms like *Pneumocystis jirovecii*, which are identified using GMS (gold standard) or PAS stains.

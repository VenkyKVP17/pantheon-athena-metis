# General Physiology and Cell QBank

## Q1. Homeostasis and Membrane Transport: Sodium-Potassium ATPase Pump Dynamics
**Question ID:** Q1785508268303
**Question:** A medical student is reviewing cell membrane physiology. The primary active transport mechanism responsible for maintaining the resting membrane potential and cellular volume balance ("getting the balance right" across the plasma membrane) pumps ions in which of the following proportions?
- A) 3 Na+ out of the cell and 2 K+ into the cell per ATP hydrolyzed
- B) 2 Na+ out of the cell and 3 K+ into the cell per ATP hydrolyzed
- C) 3 Na+ into the cell and 2 K+ out of the cell per ATP hydrolyzed
- D) 2 Na+ into the cell and 3 K+ out of the cell per ATP hydrolyzed

**Answer:** A) 3 Na+ out of the cell and 2 K+ into the cell per ATP hydrolyzed

**Explanation:**
The **Na+/K+ ATPase pump** (sodium-potassium pump) is an electrogenic primary active transporter crucial for cellular homeostasis and osmotic equilibrium.
Key physiological features:
- **Stoichiometry:** Translocates **3 Na+ ions OUT** of the cell and **2 K+ ions IN** to the cell for every molecule of ATP hydrolyzed.
- **Electrogenic Nature:** Net movement of 1 positive charge out per cycle contributes directly to the negative resting membrane potential (RMP).
- **Cell Volume Regulation:** By continuously pumping Na+ out, it prevents intracellular hyperosmolality and cellular swelling.
- **Inhibition:** Specifically inhibited by cardiac glycosides (e.g., **Ouabain**, **Digoxin**).

**Key Distinction:** Pumping 3 Na+ out vs 2 K+ in maintains both osmotic equilibrium ("cellular balance") and membrane electrical gradients.

**Take Home Message:** The Na+/K+ ATPase pump maintains resting membrane potential and cell volume balance by pumping 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in per ATP molecule hydrolyzed.

## Q2. Secondary Active Transport and Osmotic Balance: Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter (SGLT) Dynamics
**Question ID:** Q1785508281604
**Question:** A medical student is reviewing intestinal nutrient absorption and cellular ion gradients ("getting the balance right" across epithelial membranes). Which of the following transport mechanisms relies directly on the sodium gradient established by the primary active Na+/K+ ATPase to drive glucose uptake into enterocytes?
- A) Secondary active transport (symport) via SGLT1
- B) Facilitated diffusion via GLUT2
- C) Facilitated diffusion via GLUT4
- D) Primary active transport via H+/K+ ATPase

**Answer:** A) Secondary active transport (symport) via SGLT1

**Explanation:**
The **SGLT1 (Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 1)** is a classic example of **secondary active transport (symport)** across the apical membrane of enterocytes and renal proximal tubule cells.
Key physiological mechanisms:
- **Driving Force:** Does not hydrolyze ATP directly; instead, it utilizes the inward electrochemical gradient of Na+ maintained by the basolateral **Na+/K+ ATPase pump**.
- **Stoichiometry:** Cotransports 2 Na+ ions down their concentration gradient along with 1 glucose (or galactose) molecule against its concentration gradient.
- **Basolateral Exit:** Once intracellular, glucose exits into interstitial fluid across the basolateral membrane via facilitated diffusion through **GLUT2**.
- **Clinical Relevance:** Oral Rehydration Solutions (ORS) utilize Na+-coupled glucose transport via SGLT1 to stimulate solvent drag and passive water reabsorption in severe diarrhea.

**Key Distinction:** Primary active transport (Na+/K+ ATPase) creates the electrochemical potential difference that fuels secondary active transport (SGLT1).

**Take Home Message:** SGLT1 drives glucose uptake across apical enterocyte membranes via secondary active transport powered by the Na+ gradient established by the primary active Na+/K+ ATPase.

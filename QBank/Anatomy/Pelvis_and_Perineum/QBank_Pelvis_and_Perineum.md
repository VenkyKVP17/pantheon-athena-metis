# Pelvis and Perineum QBank

## Q1. Structures Related to Ischial Spine and Sciatic Foramina
**Question ID:** Q1785415429916
**Question:** Which of the following structure is not related with marked structure in image?
- A) Pudendal nerve
- B) Pudendal vessels
- C) Ischiococcygeus
- D) Tendon of obturator externus

**Answer:** D) Tendon of obturator externus

**Explanation:**
The marked structure on the pelvic bone is the **ischial spine** (and the surrounding sacrospinous ligament / lesser sciatic foramen region).

**Key Anatomical Relations of the Ischial Spine & Sacrospinous Ligament:**
1. **Pudendal nerve (S2, S3, S4):** Exits the greater sciatic foramen, crosses over the pelvic surface of the ischial spine / sacrospinous ligament, and enters the lesser sciatic foramen into the pudendal (Alcock's) canal.
2. **Internal pudendal vessels (Artery & Vein):** Accompany the pudendal nerve across the pelvic surface of the ischial spine into the lesser sciatic foramen.
3. **Ischiococcygeus (Coccygeus) muscle:** Arises from the pelvic surface of the ischial spine and sacrospinous ligament and inserts onto the lateral border of the sacrum and coccyx.
4. **Nerve to obturator internus:** Also crosses the ischial spine (Mnemonic **PIN**: **P**udendal nerve, Internal **P**udendal vessels, **N**erve to obturator internus).

**Tendon of Obturator Externus:**
- The obturator externus arises from the outer surface of the obturator membrane and surrounding bone. Its tendon passes posterior to the neck of the femur to insert into the **trochanteric fossa of the femur**. It has no relation or attachment to the ischial spine.

**Take Home Message:** The pudendal nerve, internal pudendal vessels, nerve to obturator internus, and ischiococcygeus muscle are directly related to the ischial spine and sacrospinous ligament. The tendon of obturator externus inserts into the trochanteric fossa of the femur and is unrelated to the ischial spine.

**Question:** Which of the following structure is not related with marked structure in image? (Ischial Spine)
**Options:**
A. Pudendal nerve
B. Pudendal vessels
C. Ischiococcygeus
D. Tendon of obturator externus

**Correct Answer:** D. Tendon of obturator externus

**Explanation:**
The image marks the Ischial Spine. The structures anatomically related to the ischial spine include the pudendal nerve, internal pudendal vessels, and the ischiococcygeus muscle. The tendon of the obturator externus is not related to it.

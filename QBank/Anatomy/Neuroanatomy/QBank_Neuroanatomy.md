# Neuroanatomy QBank

## Q1. Septum Pellucidum Boundary and Ventricles
**Question ID:** Q6317
**Question:** Indicated structure forms the boundary of which of the following structures?
- A) Lateral ventricles
- B) III ventricle
- C) IV ventricle
- D) Cisterna magna

**Answer:** A) Lateral ventricles

**Explanation:**
The indicated structure in the sagittal section of the brain is the **septum pellucidum**, which appears as a thin, vertical translucent membrane in the midline of the brain. It forms the medial boundary of the anterior horns of the lateral ventricles.

The septum pellucidum consists of two layers (laminae) separated by a potential space called the **cavum septi pellucidi** (also known as the fifth ventricle, though it is not a true ventricle).

**Take Home Message:** The septum pellucidum forms the medial boundary of the lateral ventricles and encloses the cavum septi pellucidi (fifth ventricle).

## Q2. Visual Scanning and Frontal Eye Field
**Question ID:** Q6317_10
**Question:** Which of the marked area in given image is involved in visual scanning?
- A) Area A
- B) Area B
- C) Area C
- D) Area D

**Answer:** A) Area A

**Explanation:**
The image shows a lateral view of the brain with four labeled areas. The question asks about the area responsible for **visual scanning**.

**Visual scanning** refers to **conjugated saccadic eye movements** — both eyes moving together from one point to another when examining a visual scene. This is controlled by the **frontal eye field (FEF)**, located in **Brodmann area 8** (Area A).

**Take Home Message:** Visual scanning (conjugated saccadic eye movement) is controlled by the Frontal Eye Field (FEF) located in Brodmann Area 8.

## Q3. Cerebellar Peduncles and Brainstem Anatomy
**Question ID:** Q6317_13
**Question:** Match the labeled structures on the posterior aspect of the brainstem and cerebellum (1-5) with their corresponding anatomical names:
a. Superior cerebellar peduncle
b. Middle cerebellar peduncle
c. Inferior cerebellar peduncle
d. Inferior colliculus

- A) a-1, b-3, c-5, d-2
- B) a-3, b-4, c-5, d-2
- C) a-3, b-5, c-4, d-1
- D) a-4, b-3, c-5, d-2

**Answer:** B) a-3, b-4, c-5, d-2

**Explanation:**
The posterior aspect of the brainstem displays key anatomical landmarks of the midbrain, pons, medulla, and cerebellar peduncles:
- **1:** Superior colliculus (tectum of midbrain)
- **2:** Inferior colliculus (tectum of midbrain, below superior colliculus)
- **3:** Superior cerebellar peduncle (brachium conjunctivum) — connects midbrain to cerebellum and forms upper lateral boundary of the fourth ventricle.
- **4:** Middle cerebellar peduncle (brachium pontis) — largest peduncle, formed by transverse pontine fibers connecting pons to cerebellum.
- **5:** Inferior cerebellar peduncle (restiform body) — connects medulla oblongata to cerebellum.

**Take Home Message:**
- Middle cerebellar peduncle (4) is the largest peduncle connecting the pons to the cerebellum.
- Superior cerebellar peduncle (3) connects midbrain to cerebellum.
- Inferior cerebellar peduncle (5) connects medulla to cerebellum.
- Inferior colliculus (2) lies on the posterior surface of the midbrain below the superior colliculus.

## Q4. Vestibulocerebellum and Balance Control (Get The Balance Right)
**Question ID:** Q6317_14
**Question:** Which anatomical division of the cerebellum is primarily responsible for maintaining equilibrium, posture, and body balance by receiving direct afferent inputs from the vestibular apparatus and vestibular nuclei?
- A) Flocculonodular lobe (Vestibulocerebellum)
- B) Anterior lobe (Spinocerebellum)
- C) Posterior lobe (Cerebrocerebellum)
- D) Vermal zone of posterior lobe

**Answer:** A) Flocculonodular lobe (Vestibulocerebellum)

**Explanation:**
The cerebellum is divided functional-anatomically into three primary lobes/functional divisions:
1. **Vestibulocerebellum (Archicerebellum):** Consists of the **flocculonodular lobe** and fastigial nucleus. It receives direct sensory input from the vestibular apparatus (semicircular canals and otolith organs: utricle and saccule) as well as the vestibular nuclei in the brainstem. Its primary roles are governing **body balance, equilibrium, gait stability, and vestibulo-ocular reflexes (VOR)**.
2. **Spinocerebellum (Paleocerebellum):** Comprises the vermis and intermediate (paravermal) zone; receives proprioceptive and spinal afferents to regulate muscle tone and execution of ongoing limb movements.
3. **Cerebrocerebellum (Neocerebellum):** Comprises the lateral cerebellar hemispheres; receives cerebral cortical input via pontine nuclei and plans complex, skilled voluntary movements.

**Take Home Message:** The vestibulocerebellum (flocculonodular lobe) is the functional division of the cerebellum dedicated to maintaining balance, equilibrium, and posture through direct vestibular connections.

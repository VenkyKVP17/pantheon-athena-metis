# Lower Limb QBank

## Q1. Gluteus Maximus Origin and Dual Action
**Question ID:** Q6317003
**Question:** Based on pelvic origin sites, which muscle originates from the posterior surface of the ilium behind the posterior gluteal line (including adjacent sacrum and coccyx) and performs dual actions of both hip extension and knee extension?
- A) Gluteus medius
- B) Gluteus maximus
- C) Gluteus minimus
- D) Semitendinosus

**Answer:** B) Gluteus maximus

**Explanation:**
Labelled pelvic areas:
- **Area A:** Posterior surface of the ilium, posterior to the posterior gluteal line, including the adjacent surfaces of the sacrum and coccyx (Origin of Gluteus Maximus).
- **Area B:** Gluteus medius origin.
- **Area C:** Ischial spine.
- **Area D:** Ischial tuberosity.

**The muscle with dual action:**
Only **gluteus maximus** performs both hip extension and knee extension:
- **Hip extension:** Gluteus maximus is the chief/primary extensor of the hip joint. Its fibers originate from Area A and pass posteriorly across the hip joint.
- **Knee extension:** While the primary function is hip extension, gluteus maximus also assists in knee extension through its insertion into the iliotibial tract (IT band). Approximately 3/4 of gluteus maximus fibers insert into the iliotibial tract.

**Take Home Message:** Gluteus maximus is the primary extensor of the hip joint and assists in knee extension via its insertion into the iliotibial tract.

## Q2. Foot Deformities and Descriptions
**Question ID:** Q6317014
**Question:** Match the following foot deformities with their corresponding descriptions:

A. Equinus
B. Planus
C. Cavus
D. Calcaneus

1. Longitudinal arch of foot flattened
2. Longitudinal arch of foot extended dorsally
3. Foot in plantar flexion
4. Foot in dorsiflexion

- A) A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1
- B) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
- C) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
- D) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3

**Answer:** B) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4

**Explanation:**
- **Equinus (3):** Foot is fixed in plantar flexion (patient walks on toes, like a horse).
- **Pes Planus (1):** Flattening of the longitudinal arch of the foot (flat foot).
- **Pes Cavus (2):** High longitudinal arch of the foot, extended dorsally (exaggerated arch).
- **Pes Calcaneus (4):** Foot is fixed in dorsiflexion (patient walks on heel).

**Take Home Message:**
- **Equinus:** Plantar flexion
- **Calcaneus:** Dorsiflexion
- **Planus:** Flattened longitudinal arch
- **Cavus:** High longitudinal arch (extended dorsally)
